Redesigning distribution planning in an enterprise ERP

RoleLead Product Designer
Timeline4 months
SectorSaaS & ERP/CRM
01Overview 02Research 03Solution 04Results
User voice
“We were forced to keep the real data in Excel and paper notes because the system wouldn’t let us see the full picture on one screen.”
Senior Distribution Manager
The main problem

75% of product distribution errors traced back to one place: data entry and reconciliation. The system meant to prevent mistakes was quietly creating them.

The goal

Give distribution managers full control over data — so errors get caught before they become financial losses, not after.

1 sprintField Observation

Shadowed distribution managers during live shifts to see where the workflow actually broke down, not where we assumed it did.

2 sprintsDeep-Dive Interviews

Sat down with distribution managers directly to unpack their day-to-day workflow and where the system fought against it.

4 sprintsDesign & Usability Testing

Tested the existing interface first to confirm where it broke, then designed the matrix in iterations and tested every prototype against the same tasks until the errors stopped.

Key Insight

The system forced a linear workflow for a process that required parallel comparison. Managers were “calculating in their heads” — so the interface had to show everything needed for one decision on one screen.

The Solution & Business Impact

Intelligent Matrix Distribution Interface

The Matrix Model

Consolidated Contracts (vertical) and Orders (horizontal) into a single interactive grid. This eliminated tab-switching and kept all variables in one viewport.

Contextual Data Cards

Integrated unit price logic (Index + Logistics – Discount) directly into the workspace, providing immediate decision-making support.

Smart Footer

Implemented a “Real-time Balance Guard”. As managers input data, the footer instantly calculates remaining volumes and prevents over-allocation.

Key Design Decision

From one record at a time to the whole picture on one screen.

BeforeLinear

One record visible at a time. Comparing orders meant switching screens, scrolling back, and holding numbers in your head — exactly where 75% of the errors came from.
